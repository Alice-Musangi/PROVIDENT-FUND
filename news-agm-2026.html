Communication & Engagement · 22 May 2026

Financial independence begins with thoughtful preparation.

Professor Gilbert Kokwaro challenged members to prepare not only for financial freedom, but also for purpose, companionship and a life of dignity after work.

AGM updatePublished 22 May 2026← Back to latest news

On Friday, 22 May 2026, members gathered for the Fund’s Annual General Meeting in an atmosphere of warmth, reflection and shared purpose. Beyond the formal proceedings, the meeting offered a powerful reminder that retirement planning is a deeply personal journey.

The highlight of the day was an inspiring and candid address by Chief Guest Professor Gilbert Kokwaro. Drawing on his own remarkable life journey, he spoke with wisdom, humility and gentle humour about the experiences that shaped his understanding of preparation and resilience.

Lessons shaped by lived experience

Professor Kokwaro recalled walking long distances to school with barely KES 20 in his pocket. He also reflected on the sobering moment when he nearly lost his university house. These experiences brought the central message into sharp focus: retirement is not a single event waiting at the end of a career. It is a lifelong process that calls for deliberate preparation.

“Retire in grace, not in grief.”

Four challenges for every member

Professor Kokwaro encouraged members to think about retirement more broadly and act intentionally:

  1. Embrace genuine education. Build a practical understanding of personal finance and retirement, and keep learning throughout your working life.
  2. Plan together. Have open and honest conversations with your spouse about where and how you hope to live in retirement.
  3. Nurture companionship. Invest in meaningful relationships that can provide belonging, connection and support in later years.
  4. Prepare with intention. Make thoughtful financial and personal choices today so retirement can be approached with confidence and dignity.

A shared commitment to prepare well

The AGM brought members together around more than Fund performance and formal accountability. It created space to reflect on the life each member is building beyond employment—and on the practical conversations, relationships and decisions that can make that future more secure.

Retirement readiness is financial, but it is also personal. The strongest plans make room for both.
